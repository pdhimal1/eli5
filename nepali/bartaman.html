🎓 Bartaman (Rice Feeding)!

The special coming of age ceremony!

What is Bartaman?

Bartaman (also called "Ritual of First Rice" or "Annaprashan") is a very special ceremony in Nepal!

It's when a baby is fed rice for the FIRST time, marking that they're growing up!

The word "Bartaman" means "remaining rice" or "full rice" - showing the child is ready to eat solid food!

When Does It Happen?

Usually when a baby is about 6-10 months old, when they can sit up and have teeth coming in!

The ceremony is often done on an auspicious day chosen by a priest (Jyotish).

🎉 How Is It Celebrated?

• The baby sits on a wooden stool (Dauso)

• The mother or grandmother feeds the baby the first bite of rice

• The priest says prayers and blessings

• Family and relatives come to celebrate

• The baby gets gifts and blessings

Why Is It Important?

In the old days, Nepal didn't have baby food in stores!

So when a baby was ready for solid food, it was a big milestone - like graduating from milk to real food!

Today, it's a way to celebrate the baby's growth and ask for blessings for their future!

🧑 What Happens After?

• The baby is now officially "grown up" enough for solid food!

• The family often donates to the poor (sharing blessings)

• Photos are taken to remember the special day

• Everyone eats delicious food and celebrates!

Different Communities

Newar: Called "Ihi" and is very elaborate with many rituals!

Brahmin/Chhetri: Usually simpler, focused on the rice feeding

Other groups: Have their own special ways of celebrating!

💭 Remember: Bartaman traditions vary by family, ethnicity, and region in Nepal. This is general information - please verify with family elders for specific customs!
